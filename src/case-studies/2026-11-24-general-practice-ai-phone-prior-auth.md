This prior authorization automation and AI phone answering case study follows a
Tennessee general practice with two physicians and a nurse practitioner. Its front
desk was fielding about 117 calls and 17 prior authorizations every business day,
and both were slipping. The results below were measured over the first 63 days.

## The Challenge

The phones and the prior-auth queue were competing for the same small staff.

- **Calls:** about 117 per business day, taking 3.76 staff hours a day to answer.
- **Abandonment:** 28% of peak-hour callers were lost, either never answered or
  hanging up in frustration after pick-up.
- **Prior auths:** about 17 per day and 13 staff hours a week. 24% missed their
  deadline, roughly four requests every day.
- **Denials:** 11% of prior-auth requests were denied.

Every missed call is a patient who may not call back, and every late or denied
authorization delays care and puts the practice's revenue at risk.

## Our Approach

We tackled the two problems separately.

**Phones: DialTone.med.** We deployed DialTone.med, our voice AI for medical
practice front desks. It answers every call, handles routine requests such as
scheduling and prescription refills, and routes clinical questions to the right
person.

**Prior auths: AuthPulse logic, delivered as a spreadsheet.** Rather than install
new software, we ran the practice's own prior-authorization data through the same
tracking logic that powers AuthPulse. The output was a working spreadsheet with
every open request, the date each payer's answer was due, and flags for requests
at risk of going late or being denied. Staff worked from that list each day.

The practice didn't need a new system to fix prior auths. It needed the deadlines
and risks it was already exposed to put in front of the people doing the work.

## Results

| Measure | Before | After 63 days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calls answered | Peak-hour calls regularly missed | 100% |
| Abandonment | 28% at peak (unanswered or after pick-up) | 7% (after pick-up only) |
| Staff time answering calls | 3.76 hours/day | About 2.4 hours/day (−37%) |
| Prior-auth deadline slippage | 24% (about 4/day) | 5% (fewer than 1/day) |
| Prior-auth denial rate | 11% | 4% |
| Staff time on prior auths | 13 hours/week | About 7.7 hours/week (−41%) |

Together, that returns roughly 12 staff hours a week to the practice: about 7
from the phones and about 5 from prior auths.

The remaining 7% abandonment is callers who hang up after the AI answers, some of
whom prefer not to speak with a voice agent. We report that number as it is
because it's the next thing to improve.
